Part one

Orientation

Verify checks a person standing in front of you against the Philippine National ID, using the PSA's eVerify service and a live face check. When the service names them, the system prints an authentication document and can issue a card the person carries on their own phone — a card a checkpoint can read later with no network at all.

One installation serves many organizations. Everything an organization owns — its counters, its transaction types, its people, its own eVerify account — belongs to that organization and to nobody else.

About the screens in this manual

Each screen is drawn from the software's own styles, at the size and in the order you will meet it. Where a number or a name appears it is the site's own sample record — Lisa Rose Reyes Cruz, 0000-1111-2222-3333 — a person who does not exist, kept precisely so a document can be looked at without a real one. No screen here shows anybody's data.

Numbered pins 1 on a screen are explained in the list directly under it.

1.1The three roles

The roles nest. Each one is a scope, and the pages you see are the scopes you hold.

Site administratorthe whole installation

Creates organizations, decides how everyone signs in, and decides which eVerify fields an organization prints and keeps. Held site-wide, whether or not the person belongs to any organization — otherwise nobody could create the first one.

Organization administratorone organization

Runs one organization's daily business: its people, its counters, its transaction types, its eVerify account, its statistics and its export. Being an admin at one organization gives you nothing at another.

Operatorone counter

Verifies people, prints documents, hands over cards, validates documents somebody else printed, and pairs their own phones.

The same person is routinely an operator at one organization and an administrator at another, so the role is a property of the pairing, not of the person. Site administration is separate again, and independent of both.

The rule behind every screen

Which organization you are working in comes from your session, never from the address bar. An organization you do not belong to does not appear to you as forbidden; it simply does not exist on your screen. This is re-checked on every request, so removing somebody's membership ends their access immediately rather than at their next sign-in.

1.2Signing in

Whereyour site's address, such as https://verify.example.org/

There are two sign-in methods and the site uses exactly one of them, chosen by a site administrator. Whichever is in use, the same rule holds: your identity comes only from what the National ID service returned, and only somebody already registered here is given a session. Nothing you type decides who you are.

By National ID and face check

  1. Read the consent notice and press Give Consent.
  2. Enter your National ID card number or your Digital National ID. You can also press Scan with Camera or Upload QR Image to read them off the card's QR.
  3. Press Start Liveness Check & Login. The National ID service opens its own camera window; follow it.
  4. The answer takes a few seconds. On success you land on the Verify page.
  1. Type either number. Both are printed on the card.
  2. Or read them off the card's QR instead of typing.
  3. Opens the National ID service's own camera window. The answer takes a few seconds.

If the site is set to National ID sign-in service instead, the page shows a single Sign in with National ID button. You are taken to the provider, checked there, and returned. Only registered operators get a session on that path too.

If sign-in fails

"An ID for the provided details could not be found." means this site has no operator registered under the number you typed. The face check still ran, and it took the same few seconds it always does — a faster refusal would tell anybody who asked whose numbers are registered here.

One real case looks like this and is fixable in a minute: a member added by card number only, who has never signed in, typing the Digital National ID printed on the same card. An administrator adds the digital ID on the People list — it is flagged there as Card number only — and it works. See 3.6.

The login page asks for no location. Signed out, the site does not yet know which organization you belong to, so it cannot know which counters to offer you. That comes next.

1.3Which organization, and which counter

Where/choose/, and the strip at the top of the Verify page

After signing in you pick the organization you are working for and the location you are sitting at. Transactions are logged against both.

If there is only one possible answer you never see this page. One organization and at most one location means there is nothing to ask, and the common case must not get slower. Otherwise: choose an organization, choose a location, press Continue.

When you can work in more than one place, a thin strip sits above the Verify page — Verifying at Window 3 — with a Change link back to the chooser. Your last choice is remembered on that computer as a convenience; it grants nothing, and your memberships are what decide what you may pick.

1.5Demo Mode

WhereSetup → User SettingsScopeYou, everywhere you go

Demo Mode redacts what is on the screen so a real ID can be shown to an audience. It is a property of the person presenting, not of the desk, so it follows you across every organization and every page — including the pages where staff are listed by name and by both ID numbers.

What it covers: the verification result and the printed document, the card handed to a phone, the offline credential inside it, the people lists, the eVerify addresses and client IDs on the settings pages, and your own name in the corner. What it does not cover: the transaction log and the CSV export. A demonstration verification really happened and is recorded truthfully; an export is a data operation, not something on a projector.

While presenting

Masked fields on settings pages are read-only, and the server refuses to save a value that still contains the redaction bullets — otherwise pressing Save during a demonstration would write bullets over the client ID the whole site signs in against. If you genuinely need to change one of those values, retype it in full and it will be accepted.

The exact conventions are in 5.1.

Part two · Operator

At the counter

2.1Verify a person

WhereVerify

Every verification starts with consent. The notice names what is collected — name, date of birth, and a biometric photo — and why. Read it to the person and press Give Consent, or Withhold Consent to stop.

Step 1 — provide the data

Three tabs, and you only need one of them.

TabUse it whenWhat you do
Scan QR Code The person has their physical or digital card. Start Camera Scan, hold the card up, Capture QR. Or Upload QR Image for a photo of the card.
Manual Entry You can read a number off the card but the QR will not scan. Type the National ID card number (1234-5678-9012-3456) or the Digital National ID.
Demographic Form There is no card at all. First name, middle name, last name, suffix if any, and birth date as mm/dd/yyyy.
  1. Always present. Opens the National ID service's own camera window.
  2. Present only when a reader serial number is set for you at this organization (2.7). Otherwise this button is not drawn at all.

Step 2 — verify the person

Press Start Face Camera & Verify. The National ID service opens its own camera window and runs the live face check; when it finishes, the lookup goes out with the face check attached.

If a fingerprint reader is configured for you at this organization, a second button, Scan Fingerprint & Verify, appears beside it. It is offered only once a serial number is set on your Setup page — see 2.7.

If the page says verification cannot run

"… has no eVerify credentials yet" means this organization's own eVerify account has not been filled in. Nothing you can do at the counter fixes it; an organization administrator adds them on the Organization page (3.5). The account is deliberately per organization, because usage bills to it.

A success takes you straight to the document page. Anything else lands on the outcome screen below.

2.2When it does not succeed

Two very different things can go wrong and they must not be confused. Either the service looked and could not confirm the person, or the lookup never happened at all. The screen tells you which, and what to do next.

What you seeWhat it meansYour next move
Cannot verify — no matching record eVerify answered: no record fits these details. Fix a typo, or start over.
Cannot verify — not confirmed A record was found and was not confirmed. Start over. Retrying returns the same answer.
Not completed — service error The lookup never reached eVerify, or eVerify refused this site's credentials. Nothing was looked up about this person. Try again with the same details. If it repeats, tell an administrator.
Not completed — camera cancelled The face check was closed before it finished. Try again with the same details.
  1. Keeps everything you typed and puts you back on the form.
  2. Keeps nothing, and drops the held face check.
  3. Offered only while a face check is held and the form has enough in it to send. It is withdrawn rather than greyed out when it cannot be used.

The screen shows what was searched for, the time, and the outcome. Two buttons close it, and they are not the same:

  • Edit Details — keeps everything you typed and puts you back on the form. Use it for a typo.
  • Start Over — keeps nothing. Use it for the next person.
The face check is held for three minutes

A wrong middle name is usually a typo, and the person who just passed the face check is still standing there. So after a failed lookup, Step 2 offers Verify Without New Face Scan with a countdown: correct the details and send them again with the same face check, no second camera run.

It is withdrawn the moment it cannot be used — when the window runs out, when the form no longer has enough in it to send, when the verification succeeds, and always when you press Start Over. Start Over means the next person, and the next person's face has not been checked.

Nothing on this page clears itself and no message disappears on a timer. Every error box has a dismiss on it; a message that vanishes while somebody is reading it is not a feature.

2.3The verification document

WhereAfter a successful verification

The result page shows the sheet exactly as it will print. Above it:

  1. Choose a transaction type — type to search the list your organization keeps. Log Transaction stays disabled until you have picked one.
  2. Press Log Transaction. This is what writes the record and mints the offline credential printed on the card.
  3. Then Print Document or Save as PDF, and — if the holder wants it on their phone — the small Send to phone code beside those buttons (2.4).

What is on the sheet

The sheet names the holder once, at the top — portrait, full name and both ID numbers — and only then lists the fields. It is drawn in the same ink as the card the holder carries on their phone, so the paper and the phone describe the person the same way.

National ID Authentication Result

The portrait, the full name on one line, and both ID numbers beneath it, closed by a blue rule. The portrait is the thing you compare against the face at the counter, so it is the last element to give way and never prints smaller than 16 mm.

Two layouts, and the sheet picks one for you

The sheet is always one A4 page. The page is measured after it is laid out, and if it does not fit, the layout changes rather than the type size.

National ID Authentication Result
PSA
The second form
An organization printing a very long list — more than about twenty-two fields — gets this one instead. The portrait moves beside the name rather than above it, and the field list runs in two columns. A long value, such as an address, still spans the full width rather than wrapping three times in a narrow column.
Why it is the fallback and not the layout
Two columns cost reading order: one column makes the order a site administrator arranged unambiguous. An organization whose list fits never sees this form.
It prints larger, not smaller
The busiest documents now print bigger than they used to: halving the row count gives back more room than shrinking the type ever did. A 36-field sheet prints at about 7.8 pt against the old 6 pt, with a 28 mm portrait against 8 mm.
A document without a card is still a document

If the offline credential could not be minted, the sheet prints without the cut-out card and its two squares. Everything else — the record, the seal, the small validation QR — is unchanged and the document is still valid. Losing the offline half is a far smaller failure than losing the record that somebody was verified.

The preview on screen is the page the printer gives you

The document on screen sits on a drawn sheet of A4, held away from the edge by the same margin the printer applies — measured off the PDF, not assumed. What you check before pressing Print Document is therefore what comes out: if the sheet looks right in the browser, it is right on paper. The two agree to within a twentieth of a millimetre.

All of it is measured on rendered pages rather than estimated, and the figures are in 5.2.

2.4Send the card to the holder's phone

Once the transaction is logged, a small QR appears beside the print buttons, labelled Send to phone — scan to save the card to Photos. The holder points their own phone camera at it, from across the desk. Nothing is typed, nothing is installed, and they do not sign in to anything.

  1. They scan it and a page opens: Your verification card.
  2. On an iPhone they press Save to Photos. On Android the same button downloads the image, and the phone's own notification opens it in the gallery.
  3. On an iPhone there is also Add to Apple Wallet, above it, when this installation is set up to issue passes. See below — it is a different object from the picture, and the holder may reasonably want both.
  4. If no button appears, they press and hold the card and choose Add to Photos (iOS) or Download image (Android). The page says so on its face.
The card in Apple Wallet

Add to Apple Wallet puts the verification in the holder's wallet, where it sits indefinitely rather than for the few minutes the link lives. It is one pass: the credential square, which a checkpoint verifies with no network, and the holder's photo. It is the same pass a holder gets from idmanager.

A pass shows exactly one barcode, so the credential square is the barcode and the face rides as the pass's picture. Where the verification carried no face, the pass has no photo.

The verifier app does not check the photo on a pass. It is the face this verification bound to the credential, but a picture on a phone can be imitated. To have the app check the face, scan the Portrait square on the saved card image or on the printed document: the app shows that face only once it matches the credential.

The button is drawn only on iPhones, and only when a site administrator has set the installation up to issue passes. Where either is not so, there is simply no button rather than one that fails when pressed. Android has no equivalent.

Five minutes, and it closes when you leave

The link is unguessable, expires within a few minutes, and is released the moment you navigate away from the document page. That is deliberate: it is the one address on this site that hands over somebody's identity without a sign-in, and the short life is most of what keeps that safe. Wait for the holder to save the card before you move on. If it lapses, log nothing again — simply reopen the document is not possible, so hand them the printed card instead.

What is on the card is decided here, not on the phone: your organization's field list, and your Demo Mode setting. A card saved while you are presenting is the redacted one.

2.5Validate a document somebody else printed

WhereValidate

Press Scan QR Code and point the camera at either the small square in the document's footer or the credential square on the cut-out card. The record comes up: who was verified, by whom, where, when, and under which transaction type. The camera gives up after thirty seconds, and Cancel stops it sooner.

The card has two squares the same size and it is easy to point at the wrong one. If you scan the portrait square the page tells you so and keeps looking — the other square is three centimetres away. There is nothing to look up in the portrait square; it carries the face, not the record.

This page needs the network. The phone does not.

Validate is the online check, and anybody with a camera can do it from the printed page. The offline check is the paired phone in 2.6, which validates the card's CL03 credential square with no connection at all.

2.6Your paired phones

WhereDevices

A paired phone running the verifier app can open a verification card with no network — at a checkpoint, in a lobby, in a field office. This site and the app together are the offline verification system, also called the CL03 implementation. Pairing, refreshing and re-keying are all done by the phone scanning a code from this page. Nothing is typed into the phone but, if a camera will not cooperate, the code is printed beside the square.

Pair a phone

  1. Name it — something you will recognise in a list, such as Field team 3 — iPhone 14.
  2. Press Pair a phone. A square and a one-time code appear, with how long they last.
  3. Scan it with the verifier app. The phone generates its own key, which never leaves it, and pulls everything else over an encrypted connection.

The code works once and expires. It carries only this server's address and a one-time password — there is no key and no personal data in the square, which is why it stays small enough to scan reliably.

The list, and what the states mean

ColumnReadsMeaning
AccessHolder detailsYour phones can read the person's details out of a card.
Document onlyYour phones can confirm a document is genuine but cannot read the details. A site administrator changes this.
StatusActivePaired and holding current keys.
Keys staleIts keys have run out or were replaced. Press Sync and scan. This is not a revocation — do not revoke a phone that only needs opening.
RevokedWithdrawn. It receives nothing further and what it holds expires.

The three buttons

  • Sync — shows a square the phone scans to pull fresh keys. Use it for Keys stale, or after a revocation elsewhere that this phone should know about.
  • Re-key — for a phone that was reinstalled, restored from a backup, or replaced its key for any reason. It rebinds the same device record to a new key, so the phone keeps its name and its history. It reads Keys stale until it syncs, which is honest: everything it held was locked to the key it just replaced.
  • Revoke — for a lost or retired handset. This cannot be undone, and a revoked phone cannot be re-keyed. Pair a new one.
Access follows you, not the handset

Whether your phones may read holder details is set on you, by a site administrator. Withdrawing it reaches every phone you own at its next sync, rather than needing each one found and edited — and a phone that may not read details is never sent the key that would let it, so the separation is cryptographic rather than a setting the app could be talked out of.

If the page warns about the address

The square carries the address this page was reached at. If that address is one the phone cannot reach — a local-only address, or a plain http one — the page says so plainly instead of drawing a code that would fail silently inside the phone. Open the site at its proper https address and generate the code again, and tell whoever runs the deployment.

2.7Your own settings

WhereSetup

Two things here are yours alone.

Demo Mode — described in 1.5. One switch, following you everywhere.

Fingerprint reader — the reader attached to the workstation you sit at. Fill in the reader's WebAPI endpoint (a full URL including http:// or https://) and its serial number. Leave the serial as 0 when there is no reader: the Verify page offers the fingerprint button only once a serial number is set.

The reader belongs to you at this organization. If you also work at another organization, its reader is a separate setting — switch there and come back to this page. The section only appears once you are inside an organization.

Part three · Organization administrator

Running an organization

3.1What you administer

WhereOrganizationScopeThe organization you are currently working in

The Organization tab edits the organization your session is in, and only that one. If you administer two, switch context (1.3) and the tab follows you. Each section on the page collapses; open the one you need.

You own the daily business: statistics, the export, the organization's appearance, its eVerify account, its people, its counters and its transaction types. Two things are deliberately not yours, and both belong to a site administrator: which fields are printed and kept (4.4), and how the whole site signs in (4.6).

3.2Statistics

Four counts for a date range you choose: people in the organization, verifications run, those that succeeded, and those that did not. Set Start Date and End Date and press Filter; Clear puts it back to everything.

3.3Export the transaction log

Choose a date range and press Export. You get a CSV named for the organization and the range, holding one row per verification.

Every row starts with the facts that make it a log entry — the transaction ID, the organization, the operator's National ID number, the location, and whether it was run by a site administrator without a membership here — then the person's fields, then the timestamp, the result and the transaction type.

Two things to know before wiring this into something

The columns follow the document's order, not a fixed one, and a site administrator can change it. Read the file by its header row, never by column position.

The export is never redacted, even while you are in Demo Mode. It is a data operation, so treat the file as the personal data it is.

3.4Profile and mark

The organization's name heads its banner and prints on every document and inside every credential. A banner subtitle is optional. A logo can be uploaded; without one, the organization's initials are drawn on a coloured square in the mark colour you pick.

Logos are stored as image data in the organization's own record rather than as a file on a server, so they survive a redeploy and appear identically on every instance. SVG is refused: it can carry script, and this image is printed into a document that leaves the building.

Renaming is safe at any time — the organization's record already owns everything that points at it. Credentials already issued keep the name they were issued under, which is the more truthful answer for a signed document.

3.5eVerify credentials

This is the account your verifications run against, and bill to. Base URL, Client ID, Client Secret and Public API Key. Secrets already saved are shown as Stored — leave blank to keep: they are never sent back to a browser, so leaving the box empty keeps what is stored and typing in it replaces it.

There is no fallback here, deliberately

An organization with no eVerify account refuses to verify, and says so by name on the Verify page. It does not quietly borrow another organization's account: that would bill one organization's verifications to another, and would make a missing-credentials mistake look exactly like everything working.

The account used to sign operators in is a different one, set site-wide (4.6). Sign-in happens before anybody has an organization, so there is no organization yet whose account could be used.

3.6People

Members are added by National ID. Enter the National ID card number, optionally the Digital National ID, choose Operator or Admin, and press Add. Search the list by name or by either number; change somebody's role from the dropdown on their row.

  1. This person cannot sign in with the Digital National ID on their card until somebody adds it here. It is the first thing to check when a new member says sign-in refuses them.
The "Card number only" flag

A member added by card number alone, who has never signed in, cannot sign in with the Digital National ID printed on the same card — the site has not yet been told the two belong together. The list flags them, and adding the digital ID here fixes it. This is worth checking the first time a new member says sign-in does not work.

Remove asks for confirmation and then removes the person from this organization. Their account is untouched: a person is site-wide and routinely belongs to another organization. Their past transactions stay in your log, as they must.

3.7Locations and transaction types

Two lists, both yours, both edited the same way: type a name, press Add; click a name to rename it; Remove to drop it. Both are searchable and paged.

  • Locations are the counters your operators sit at. They are what /choose/ offers and what each transaction is logged against.
  • Transaction types are what a verification was for. An operator must pick one before Log Transaction will do anything, so this list is worth keeping short and worth keeping in the words your staff actually use.

These lists live on this page and nowhere else — deliberately, so there is only ever one screen that owns each. A site administrator sees their counts but does not edit them.

Part four · Site administrator

Running the installation

4.1The Site page

WhereSite

Four counts across the top — organizations, how many are enabled, people, and verifications — then one row per organization: its people, locations and transaction type counts, whether its eVerify account is Configured or shows No credentials, an Enabled switch, and Manage.

  1. Delete is not offered here: this organization has verified people, and deleting it would leave those transactions and credentials belonging to nobody. The row says so instead of showing a button the server would refuse.
  2. This organization cannot verify anybody until its own eVerify account is filled in. Its operators see a red banner on the Verify page saying so by name.
  3. Disabled. It keeps everything, stops appearing at sign-in, and any session inside it ends on the next request.

The Enabled switch takes effect at once. A disabled organization stops appearing at sign-in, and any session already inside it ends on the next request rather than at the next login.

4.2Create an organization

Create organization opens a form. Nothing exists until you submit it — you can open the page, look at it, and leave without creating anything. Give it a name at minimum; an issuer code is assigned when it is created, and it is what identifies the organization inside a credential it mints.

The sections that need a saved organization — its people, its eVerify field list, the copy-credentials control — appear only after the first save. If the form is refused it hands your typing back rather than emptying itself.

4.4Which eVerify fields an organization keeps

WhereSite → Manage → eVerify fields

Every field a Tier II lookup returns, in one list, with two switches each.

  • Display puts the field on the verification document — and therefore in the offline credential and on the holder's card, which carry what the document shows.
  • Log records it in the transaction log, and so in the export.
  • A field with both off is never asked for at all.
  1. The photograph has no Log switch at all — not one that is off. An image is never written to a log column.
  2. A field below the divider shows its raw eVerify key until you give it a name. Switch it on without naming it and the document prints present_barangay.

One order governs both: the document prints its rows in it and the export takes its columns from it, skipping what is not logged. Drag a row, or use the arrows. The name you give a field is what the document prints and what the CSV column is called; the first thirteen came with names, the rest show their raw eVerify key until you give them one. A preview of the document rows and the CSV header row sits beside the list and updates as you work. Save fields applies it; Reset to defaults puts the whole list back.

"Log" off means never recorded

Not merely unexported. The value is not kept at all, so it cannot be exported later by switching the toggle back on. History already recorded is untouched, but the gap while a field was off stays a gap — including on the Validate page and in the organization's transaction list.

Why this is yours and not the organization's

An organization's counters and transaction types are its daily business. What is printed on a document that leaves the building, and what is kept in a log afterwards, is a decision about the deployment. One screen owns each.

The face photo has no Log switch at all — it is an image, and it is never written to a log column. The list itself is fixed: adding a key is a change to the software, not a setting.

4.6How the site signs in

WhereSetup → Site Administration

This section appears on the Setup page for site administrators only, and it applies to every organization on the installation.

Credential validity

How long a new document's cut-out card and a holder's credential stay valid, in days: 30 unless you change it, and up to 365. The date prints on the card and is signed into its code, so changing this never moves the date on a card already issued. A phone that syncs can read a card until it expires. A longer validity therefore puts more keys on every phone for longer, and a lost phone can open more old cards.

Login method

Two, and exactly one is live at a time:

  • eVerify — the default. The login page runs the National ID lookup and the face check itself, using the sign-in account below.
  • Single sign-on — an OpenID Connect provider the site administrator names runs the lookup and the face check on its side and returns the identifiers. Requires an SSO Issuer, Client ID and Client Secret; selecting the method with any of those missing is refused at save time, so the site cannot be left with a login route that has no provider behind it.

Registering this site with the provider is a separate step done by whoever runs the provider. The redirect address is exact-match and is https://<host>/national-id/callback/.

Sign-in eVerify client

The account used to sign operators in. It is site-wide because the login page runs before anybody has a session, so the site does not yet know which organization the person belongs to and cannot know whose account to use. It is a different account from the ones organizations verify with (3.5), and merging them is not possible.

Check this before deploying anything

If the sign-in account is wrong or empty, nobody can sign in — including you. Change it during a quiet hour, and confirm with a real sign-in afterwards rather than by reading the form back.

4.7Who may read holder details on a phone

WhereDjango admin → Users

A paired phone either confirms a document is genuine, or additionally reads the holder's details out of it. That is set on the person, not the handset: a self-service page where somebody granted their own phone access would not be a control at all, and holding it on the person means withdrawing it reaches every phone they own at that phone's next sync.

It is on by default for an organization's members, because a verifier that cannot show the name it has just validated cannot be compared against the person holding the card — and somebody trusted to run the verification on the web is already reading the same fields there. Withdraw it on the user when somebody should not have it.

Device records themselves are read-only in the admin. A row describes a phone that presented a real key; editing one by hand could only make it describe a phone that does not exist.

4.8Issued credentials and revocation

WhereDjango admin → Issued credentials

Every offline credential this installation mints is recorded. The list is read-only and cannot be added to by hand — it is the authoritative record of what exists in the world, and a credential that exists but is not listed here could never be withdrawn.

Revoking one is done from this list. Paired phones pick the withdrawal up at their next sync, so a phone that has not synced recently may still accept a revoked document — which is the reason Sync exists on the Devices page and the reason a fleet should sync on a schedule.

There is no un-revoke

A credential withdrawn and then quietly restored is worse than one never withdrawn: the phone that cached the revocation and the phone that did not would disagree about the same document. If a document was revoked in error, verify the person again and issue a new one.

4.9Refused sign-in attempts

WhereDjango admin → Login attempts

When somebody attempts to sign in with a National ID this site does not know, the attempt is recorded — the number used, the time, and the frame the face check handed back. Successful sign-ins are not recorded here, and a refused attempt is not a transaction: a stranger at the login page has no organization and must not appear in one.

Treat this table as sensitive, and age it out

It holds photographs of people, some of whom are simply your own staff who mistyped a digit. Nothing empties it automatically — how long the log is worth keeping is a policy decision for whoever runs the deployment, not a default this software should pick. The maintenance command purge_login_attempts --days 90 clears it, and --photos-only keeps who-tried-with-what while dropping the faces.

It is the first place to look when an operator reports that sign-in refuses them: the number they actually typed is in the row, which usually settles it in one glance.

The squares are sized so no printed module falls below 0.4 mm, which is where a laser print photographed under ordinary lighting stops scanning reliably. The card reads sideways until it is cut out, which is exactly what lets it fit on the sheet. A phone scanning it can be held at any angle.

How one page is guaranteed

The space the card needs below the cut line is reserved, not merely preferred — nothing above it can push it onto a second sheet. The block above is then laid out, measured, and reduced only as far as it needs to be, with a floor under every element that gives way: type never goes below 5.5 pt and the portrait never below 16 mm. Why the busiest sheet is not the smallest print

A 36-field document prints larger than a 22-field one. Running the list in two columns halves the number of rows, which gives back far more room than shrinking the type ever did — so the sheet that had to give up the most is the one that gained the most when the layout changed rather than the size.

If a symbol will not scan

The constraint is almost always the camera, not the printer — draft quality on plain paper decodes about as reliably as photo quality on coated stock. What matters is how many pixels the camera puts on each module: fill the frame with the one square you are scanning rather than photographing the whole page.

5.4Glossary

eVerifyThe PSA service this site checks people against. Two separate accounts are used: one site-wide for signing operators in, one per organization for verifying the public.
Liveness / face checkThe camera step run by eVerify's own software, which confirms a live person is present. This site never opens the camera itself.
TransactionOne logged verification: who was checked, by whom, where, when, for what, and whether it succeeded.
Transaction typeWhat a verification was for. Each organization keeps its own list.
CredentialThe signed, encrypted record printed in the card's larger square, as a CL03 code. A paired phone opens it with no network. Once issued it cannot be edited — only revoked.
CL03The credential square's format, and the way a paired phone validates it offline. Its text is the transaction number, then CL03: and the signed record. The offline verification system that reads it is the CL03 implementation.
CardThe two-square object below the document's cut line, and the same object saved to a holder's phone.
Wallet passThe same verification added to a holder's Apple Wallet on an iPhone, as one pass: the credential square, with the holder's photo. The verifier app checks the face from the portrait square on the card or the document, not from the pass.
Issuer codeA short code assigned to an organization when it is created, identifying it inside the credentials it mints.
MembershipOne person's role — Operator or Admin — inside one organization. The same person can hold different roles at different organizations.
Site administratorA site-wide capability, held independently of any membership.
Demo ModeA per-person switch that redacts what is on screen so a real ID can be shown to an audience. It never changes what is recorded.
